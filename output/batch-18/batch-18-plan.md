### M18001 | Loyal Employees Tested → The Retiring Owner Was Watching All Along

**Source:** concepts4 / E07 Test / Secret Evaluator #21  
**Engine:** E07 — Test / Secret Evaluator  
**Opening Style:** Moral Choice

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (The test begins):** A stranger walks into a family-owned printing company in Milwaukee on a Monday morning and introduces himself as the new owner. He's abrasive, dismissive, and immediately demands the staff cut corners on a long-standing client's order to save costs. The real owner — Frank Novak, 68, retiring after forty-one years — is watching remotely from his home office on a security feed.
2. **Build (Choices diverge):** Over five days, the "new boss" escalates. He insults the warehouse manager's pace. He tells the design lead her ideas are outdated. He orders the shipping clerk to re-label a product grade. Some employees comply silently. But three refuse: the warehouse manager says "We don't ship what we wouldn't sign," the design lead pushes back on a change that would breach a client promise, and the shipping clerk puts his badge on the desk and says he'll quit before he lies on a label.
3. **Turn (Evaluator reveals):** On Friday afternoon, Frank Novak walks in the front door. The stranger stands aside. Frank faces the full staff and explains: the "new boss" was an actor, and the week was a test. Not of productivity — of integrity. "I needed to know who keeps this company honest when I'm gone."
4. **Payoff (Earned reward):** The three who refused are named in the succession plan. The warehouse manager becomes operations director. The design lead gets the creative seat at the table. The shipping clerk — the one who put his badge down — is given the badge back and promoted to floor supervisor. Frank's retirement letter, read aloud, says: "A company is only as good as the people who say no when saying yes is easier."

**Character Lock:**
- Frank Novak — 68, Polish American, stocky, silver crew cut, reading glasses on a lanyard, watching from home on a laptop, printing company owner for 41 years
- "Daniel Voss" (the actor) — 45, sharp suit, slicked hair, commanding voice, planted as the abrasive new owner
- Ray Okafor — 52, Black male, warehouse manager, work boots and company polo, steady hands, the one who says "We don't ship what we wouldn't sign"
- Setting: Family-owned printing company, industrial district, Milwaukee WI, mid-October, warehouse floor + open-plan office
- Object clue: the shipping clerk's badge placed on the desk — the moment of refusal

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Ray stands at the warehouse loading dock with arms crossed, facing "Daniel Voss" who points at a pallet of product with an aggressive stance — other workers visible in the background pausing to watch, a shipping label clipboard between them on the counter.

### M18002 | Retired Groundskeeper's Bench Removed → He's the Anonymous Donor Who Funded the Park

**Source:** concepts3 / Retirement & Late-Life Reckoning #13  
**Engine:** E00 — Humiliation → Reveal  
**Opening Style:** Silent Witness

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (Humiliation):** A parks department crew in Asheville, North Carolina removes a personal bench from the public garden without notice. The bench — hand-built from cedar, with a small brass plaque — belonged to a retired groundskeeper named Willis Tate who tended this park for thirty-four years. The crew chief calls it "unauthorized property on public land."
2. **Build (Clue):** Willis, 71, arrives that morning for his daily walk and finds the bench gone. He says nothing. He simply stands where it was, hands in his jacket pockets, looking at the empty patch of gravel. A young park ranger watches from across the path. She's noticed the plaque before but never read it closely. Now she's curious why a bench removal requires three men and a truck.
3. **Turn (Recognition):** The ranger retrieves the bench from the storage yard and reads the plaque for the first time. It doesn't say "In Memory Of" — it says "Donated by" followed by a trust name. She runs the trust name through county records. The trust funded the park's $2.3 million renovation in 2009 — new paths, the water feature, the children's garden. The trust's sole trustee: Willis James Tate.
4. **Payoff (Consequence):** The parks director receives a call from the ranger. Then from the county administrator. Then from a local journalist. The bench is returned within forty-eight hours, bolted permanently to a concrete pad with a new, larger plaque. The crew chief is reassigned. Willis doesn't attend the re-installation. He just walks past it the next morning, tips his cap, and continues his route.

**Character Lock:**
- Willis Tate — 71, Black male, lean and quiet, gray beard, olive field jacket, canvas hat, walks with a cane he doesn't need, former groundskeeper turned anonymous benefactor
- Crew chief (unnamed) — 40s, parks department polo, clipboard, dismissive tone
- Ranger Sofia Delgado — 29, Latina, green uniform, observant, the one who investigates
- Setting: Public botanical garden, Asheville NC, early morning, October mist, gravel paths, the empty patch where the bench was
- Object clue: the brass plaque on the cedar bench — bearing the trust name that funded the park

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Willis stands motionless on the gravel path where his bench used to be — hands in his jacket pockets, looking down at the empty space, morning mist around him, the park's water feature visible in the background — while a parks department truck with the bench loaded drives away behind him.

### M18003 | Dropped His Old Friends for Wealth → Ruined and Alone, No One Answers

**Source:** concepts4 / E20 The Cost of Pride #6  
**Engine:** E20 — The Cost of Pride  
**Opening Style:** Dialogue Slap

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (The proud rejection):** At a rooftop bar in Atlanta, Craig Pemberton — 38, newly flush from a tech acquisition — tells his three closest friends from college that he's "moving on." He doesn't use those words exactly. He says: "I need people around me who are going somewhere." He picks up the tab as if generosity excuses the dismissal. His friends sit in silence. One of them — Marcus — reaches for his coat and leaves without finishing his drink.
2. **Build (The climb and cost):** Over four years, Craig builds a lifestyle: new city, new circle, investors and influencers. He ghosts birthdays. Ignores Marcus's wedding invite. Lets his college roommate's calls go to voicemail until they stop. He tells himself momentum requires trimming weight. His new friends toast him at parties. None of them have his spare key.
3. **Turn (The fall):** A failed Series B, a lawsuit from a former partner, and a public SEC filing strip Craig of everything in eleven months. His new circle evaporates in weeks — they were there for the rooftop, not the man. He sits in a studio apartment with a phone full of contacts and no one who will answer at 2 AM.
4. **Payoff (Self-inflicted, no rescue):** Craig calls Marcus. The number is disconnected — Marcus moved, changed carriers, moved on. He texts his old roommate. Read, no reply. He finds his college group chat archived on his phone. The last message was four years ago: "We'll miss you, man. Door's always open." The door was open. He bricked it shut himself. No one comes. No rescue. He learns what "going somewhere" costs when you go alone.

**Character Lock:**
- Craig Pemberton — 34→38, white male, sharp jawline, expensive watch (later pawned), tailored jacket → wrinkled henley in a bare apartment
- Marcus Webb — 34, Black male, Craig's closest college friend, quiet dignity, the one who leaves the rooftop bar first
- Setting: Rooftop bar in Midtown Atlanta (opening) → bare studio apartment with moving boxes (ending), four-year span
- Object clue: the unanswered phone — screen showing "Marcus Webb: Number not in service"

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Craig leans back in his chair at the rooftop bar, drink raised, speaking confidently — while across the table his three friends sit in visible silence, Marcus already reaching for his coat, the Atlanta skyline lit behind them.

### M18004 | Charity Kettle Knocked Over → The Philanthropist Was Watching Who Helped

**Source:** concepts4 / E07 Test / Secret Evaluator #26  
**Engine:** E07 — Test / Secret Evaluator  
**Opening Style:** Silent Witness

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (The test is set):** Outside a grocery store in Des Moines on a December afternoon, a man in a plain coat drops a hundred-dollar bill into a Salvation Army kettle. Then he stumbles — nudging the kettle's tripod with his elbow. The red bucket tips, spilling coins and bills across the wet sidewalk. The bell-ringer scrambles. The man steps back, apologizes, and watches.
2. **Build (Choices emerge):** Shoppers stream past. Some glance down at the scattered money and keep walking. One woman steps over a twenty-dollar bill. A teenager pockets a five and disappears. But a grocery clerk on his break — still wearing his store apron — drops to his knees and begins gathering every coin, crawling under a parked shopping cart to retrieve a rolled quarter. A mother and her young son join him. The three of them spend six minutes restoring every dollar to the bucket.
3. **Turn (The evaluator reveals himself):** The man in the plain coat returns. He doesn't look like what he is — Lawrence Egan, 72, the founder of a regional grocery chain and a $40 million charitable trust. He asks the bell-ringer for the clerk's name. Then he walks into the store, finds the clerk in the break room, and introduces himself.
4. **Payoff (Earned reward):** The clerk — Javier, 24, working two jobs to finish community college — receives a full-ride scholarship through the Egan Trust, effective January. The letter arrives two weeks later. It says: "I test people with small moments. You were the one who knelt." The teenager who pocketed the five is never confronted. No punishment. The reward is the story.

**Character Lock:**
- Lawrence Egan — 72, white male, plain gray overcoat, no jewelry, scuffed shoes, deliberate ordinariness, grocery chain founder and philanthropist
- Javier Morales — 24, Latino, store apron over a hoodie, name tag visible, on his break, kneeling on wet pavement
- Setting: Grocery store entrance, suburban Des Moines IA, December afternoon, gray sky, red Salvation Army kettle on its tripod, wet sidewalk, holiday shoppers
- Object clue: the spilled coins on wet pavement — and Javier's knees on the concrete

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Javier kneels on the wet sidewalk in his store apron, hands cupping scattered coins near a tipped-over red kettle — holiday shoppers walking past in the background, and a man in a plain gray coat standing ten feet away, watching with hands in his pockets.

### M18005 | Girl Donates Her Teddy Bear to a Hospital → Decades Later It Comforts Her Own Child

**Source:** concepts4 / E18 Gift Comes Full Circle #14  
**Engine:** E18 — Gift Comes Full Circle  
**Opening Style:** Object Clue

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (The gift given):** In 1997, a seven-year-old girl named Amara Osei walks into a children's hospital in Richmond, Virginia, and places her stuffed bear — a worn brown teddy named Captain — into a donation bin. Her mother is being treated upstairs. A nurse tags it and places it on the comfort cart. Amara goes home without Captain for the first time in her life.
2. **Build (The journey):** Captain travels the pediatric ward for twenty-six years. He's re-stitched four times. His left ear is replaced. A volunteer sews a small heart patch over a tear in his chest. He sits with children during MRIs, blood draws, first nights alone. His tag fades. No one remembers who donated him. He becomes simply "the bear on the cart" — the one kids always reach for first.
3. **Turn (It returns):** In 2023, Amara — now 33, a pediatric social worker at a different hospital — brings her own daughter, age five, to Richmond Children's for a tonsillectomy. The girl is frightened. A volunteer offers the comfort cart. The girl reaches past three newer toys and pulls out a faded brown bear with a heart patch and one replaced ear. Amara freezes. She turns the bear over. On the bottom of his left foot, in faded permanent marker, are three letters: A-M-A.
4. **Payoff (Full circle, poetic):** Amara holds the bear and her daughter at the same time. The nurse asks if something's wrong. Amara shakes her head. "I gave him away in this building twenty-six years ago. He just came back." The daughter names him Captain without being told. Amara doesn't correct her. She doesn't need to. The gift completed its circle — it left a scared girl's hands and returned to comfort her daughter in the same hallway, in the same hospital, one generation later.

**Character Lock:**
- Amara Osei — 7 (1997) → 33 (2023), Black female, braids as a child → natural locs as an adult, pediatric social worker, protective mother
- Lily Osei — 5, Amara's daughter, hospital gown, wide eyes, clutching the bear
- Setting: Children's Hospital of Richmond VA, pediatric wing hallway, 2023, fluorescent lights, comfort cart in the corridor, colorful murals on walls
- Object clue: the faded brown teddy bear with a heart patch, one replaced ear, and "A-M-A" in marker on the left foot

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** A young girl in a hospital gown sits on a gurney clutching a worn brown teddy bear with a heart patch — her mother standing beside her, hand frozen mid-reach, staring at the bear with an expression of stunned recognition, the hospital comfort cart visible in the background.

### M18006 | "Empty Chair" Award for a Dropout → She Appears in Scrubs, GED + Nursing Degree Done

**Source:** concepts3 / Graduation, Ceremony & Public Honor #5  
**Engine:** E00 — Humiliation → Reveal  
**Opening Style:** Accuser POV

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (Humiliation):** At a high school awards ceremony in Tulsa, Oklahoma, a veteran English teacher named Mrs. Faulkner presents an "Empty Chair Award" — a symbolic recognition of students who left. She places a folded certificate on an empty seat in the front row and says: "This is for Keisha Williams, who left us junior year. Wherever she is, we hope she's well." The audience murmurs. Some parents exchange glances. The gesture reads as pity, not honor.
2. **Build (Clue):** From the back of the auditorium, behind the last row of parents, a woman stands. She's wearing navy blue scrubs and white sneakers, a hospital ID badge clipped to her pocket. Her hair is pulled back. She's twenty years old. She doesn't move toward the stage immediately. She waits — watching Mrs. Faulkner set the certificate on the empty chair.
3. **Turn (Recognition):** Keisha walks down the center aisle. The room goes quiet. Mrs. Faulkner turns from the podium. Keisha reaches the front row, picks up the certificate, and says into the silence: "I got my GED four months after I left. I finished nursing school in three years. I start at St. Francis next week." She holds up her hospital ID badge. The auditorium erupts.
4. **Payoff (Consequence):** Mrs. Faulkner steps down from the podium and embraces her. The "Empty Chair Award" — intended as a kind but patronizing gesture — becomes the most shared moment of the ceremony. Keisha didn't come back to prove anyone wrong. She came back because Mrs. Faulkner was the only teacher who called her after she dropped out. Every week. For a year. The certificate is reissued with her full title: Keisha Williams, RN.

**Character Lock:**
- Keisha Williams — 20, Black female, navy scrubs, white sneakers, hospital ID badge clipped to pocket, hair pulled back in a bun, composed and steady
- Mrs. Faulkner — 58, white female, reading glasses, formal blazer, emotional at the podium, the teacher who never stopped calling
- Setting: High school auditorium, Tulsa OK, May evening, folding chairs, stage with podium, one empty chair in the front row with a folded certificate
- Object clue: the hospital ID badge on Keisha's scrubs — and the folded certificate on the empty chair

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Mrs. Faulkner stands at the podium looking down at a folded certificate placed on an empty chair in the front row — the auditorium full of seated parents and students, and at the very back, a young woman in navy scrubs is standing, visible above the seated crowd.

### M18007 | He Took His Wife for Granted → She Built a Life Without Him

**Source:** concepts4 / E20 The Cost of Pride #15  
**Engine:** E20 — The Cost of Pride  
**Opening Style:** Room Goes Wrong

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (The proud assumption):** For fourteen years, Derek Callahan treats his wife, Noor, as permanent furniture. He cancels date nights for golf. He forgets anniversaries without apology. When she finishes her master's degree at forty-one, he says "That's nice" without looking up from his phone. He is certain — in the way only an unexamined man can be — that she will always be there when he turns around.
2. **Build (She builds quietly):** Noor doesn't fight. She doesn't threaten. She just begins to grow where she's planted without him. She builds a consulting practice from the kitchen table. She makes friends he never meets. She starts running half-marathons. She stops asking him to come to things. He notices none of it. He interprets her silence as contentment. It's not. It's preparation.
3. **Turn (The departure):** On a Tuesday in March, Derek comes home to a clean house, a letter on the counter, and a closet half-empty. The letter is one page: "I waited for you to see me. You never did. I'm not angry. I'm just done." No argument. No drama. She signed the lease on her own apartment three months ago. He didn't know she was looking.
4. **Payoff (Self-inflicted, no rescue):** Six months later, Derek sees Noor at a mutual friend's birthday. She's laughing. She's with people he doesn't recognize. She looks lighter. She waves politely — the way you wave at a former neighbor. He realizes he isn't the villain of her story anymore. He's not even a character in it. She didn't leave in anger. She left because she outgrew a man who never grew at all. And no one is coming to make him feel better about it.

**Character Lock:**
- Derek Callahan — 44, white male, broad-shouldered, golf polo, phone always in hand, oblivious confidence that curdles into confusion
- Noor Callahan (née Hadid) — 42, Lebanese American, warm brown skin, black hair now cut shorter, growing from a kitchen-table laptop to a polished consultant, quiet strength
- Setting: Suburban home in Charlotte NC (opening/middle) → mutual friend's party (ending), span of ~1 year
- Object clue: the one-page letter on the kitchen counter — and the half-empty closet

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Derek stands in the kitchen holding a single folded letter, staring at the open closet door down the hallway where empty hangers are visible — the house perfectly clean, afternoon light through the window, no other person in the frame.

### M18008 | A Clerk Kneels for a Lost Child → The Store Owner Was Testing Character

**Source:** concepts4 / E07 Test / Secret Evaluator #4  
**Engine:** E07 — Test / Secret Evaluator  
**Opening Style:** Moral Choice

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (The test is set):** In a big-box home improvement store in Orlando, Florida, a small girl — maybe four years old — stands alone in the lighting aisle, clutching a stuffed rabbit, lower lip trembling. She's been there for ninety seconds. Three employees have walked past. One glanced and kept moving. Another said "not my department" into his radio. A third didn't look up from his inventory scanner.
2. **Build (The choice):** A fourth employee — Tamika Ross, 28, garden department, technically on the opposite end of the store — spots the girl from the end of the aisle. She's carrying a flat of seedlings to restock. She sets the flat down on the floor, walks over, and kneels. Eye level. "Hey, sweetie. Are you looking for your mom? I'm Tamika. I work here. Can I help you find her?" The girl nods. Tamika takes her hand and walks her to customer service. It takes four minutes.
3. **Turn (The evaluator reveals):** The girl is not lost. Her mother is around the corner, watching. And so is someone else — a woman in plain clothes with a notepad, observing from behind an endcap display. She is Diane Prescott, 61, the founder and majority owner of the regional franchise group. She's been running this test across twelve stores this quarter. She's looking for one thing: who kneels.
4. **Payoff (Earned reward / earned loss):** Tamika is called to the district office the following week. She expects a write-up for leaving her section. Instead, Diane is there. "You're the only one in this store who stopped. The only one who got on her level. That's not trainable." Tamika is offered an assistant manager position and a leadership development track. The three who walked past face no punishment — but they aren't offered anything either. The test's result speaks for itself.

**Character Lock:**
- Tamika Ross — 28, Black female, garden department apron with dirt on the knees, box braids pulled back, warm voice, carrying a flat of seedlings she sets down
- Diane Prescott — 61, white female, plain clothes (khakis, reading glasses on a chain), notepad, observing from behind an endcap, franchise owner
- The little girl — 4, planted participant, stuffed rabbit, lower lip trembling, standing alone in the lighting aisle
- Setting: Big-box home improvement store, Orlando FL, weekday midmorning, fluorescent lights, wide aisles, lighting displays overhead
- Object clue: the flat of seedlings Tamika sets on the floor — the moment she chooses the child over her task

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Tamika kneels in the store aisle at eye level with a small girl holding a stuffed rabbit — the flat of seedlings abandoned on the floor behind her, fluorescent lights overhead, the aisle stretching empty in both directions, no other employee visible.

### M18009 | Old Angler Gives a Kid His Tackle Box → The Kid Returns to Take Him Fishing One Last Time

**Source:** concepts4 / E18 Gift Comes Full Circle #23  
**Engine:** E18 — Gift Comes Full Circle  
**Opening Style:** Letter Fragment

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (The gift given):** In the summer of 2006, on a dock in Lake Hartwell, South Carolina, a retired electrician named Bud Claiborne gives his tackle box to a twelve-year-old boy fishing with a stick and a hook. The box is green metal, dented, heavy with forty years of lures and weights. Bud says: "You'll get more use out of it than I will." The boy — Dante — carries it home like treasure. They never exchange last names.
2. **Build (The object's journey):** Dante fishes with that tackle box through high school, through college, through a move to Atlanta and a career in civil engineering. He replaces the lures. Adds his own. The box rusts at the hinges. He oils them. He never buys a new one. He tells his wife: "An old man gave me this when I had nothing. It's the reason I fish." He doesn't know if Bud is alive. He's never looked.
3. **Turn (The return):** In 2024, Dante brings his son to Lake Hartwell for the first time — returning to the same dock. An old man is sitting there. Ninety-one years old, oxygen tank beside him, fishing hat faded to nothing. Dante's hands go cold. He looks at the tackle box in his own hand. He looks at the man. He says: "You gave me this. Summer of 2006. I was twelve." Bud squints. Then he smiles. "Green box? Heavy as hell?" Dante nods. "I wondered if you'd keep it."
4. **Payoff (Full circle, poetic):** Dante opens the box and hands Bud a rod. They fish together — the old man, the grown boy, and Dante's son — for three hours on the same dock. Bud catches nothing. Dante catches a bass. Bud says: "That's my box catching those." It's the last season Bud fishes. He dies that November. Dante keeps the box on his mantel. His son asks to use it one day. Dante says: "When you find a kid who needs it more than you do."

**Character Lock:**
- Bud Claiborne — 73 (2006) → 91 (2024), white male, retired electrician, oxygen tank, faded fishing hat, thin arms, sitting on the dock like he always has
- Dante Williams — 12 (2006) → 30 (2024), Black male, grew from a barefoot kid with a stick-and-hook to a broad-shouldered engineer, carries the same green tackle box
- Dante's son — 7, beside his father, holding a child's rod, watching
- Setting: Wooden dock on Lake Hartwell SC, summer morning, pine trees, still water, two-decade span (2006 → 2024)
- Object clue: the green metal tackle box — rusted hinges, forty years of lures, passed from one generation's hands to the next

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Dante stands at the edge of the wooden dock holding the battered green tackle box, looking at an old man in a fishing hat sitting at the end of the dock with an oxygen tank beside him — the lake still and pine-lined, a small boy visible behind Dante holding a child's rod.

### M18010 | Foster Child Punished for Drawing Siblings → It's the Only Image of All Six Together

**Source:** concepts3 / Adoption, Foster Care & Orphanage #15  
**Engine:** E00 — Humiliation → Reveal  
**Opening Style:** Dialogue Slap

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (Humiliation):** In a foster home in Columbus, Ohio, a nine-year-old girl named Nia is pulled from her bedroom by the foster mother and marched to the hallway where three other foster children stand watching. On the bedroom wall, in crayon and marker, Nia has drawn six figures holding hands. The foster mother points at the wall and says: "This is property damage. You're paying for this with your allowance. All of it."
2. **Build (Clue):** Nia doesn't cry. She doesn't explain. She just looks at the wall. The six figures are different heights, different hair, different colors — but they're all holding hands. Each one has a name written underneath in a child's careful printing. A visiting child welfare advocate — in the home for a routine check — notices the drawing through the open bedroom door. She steps closer. She counts the names: six. She asks the foster mother how many siblings Nia has. The foster mother shrugs. "I don't know. We just got her."
3. **Turn (Recognition):** The advocate photographs the drawing with her phone. She runs the names through the state's placement system that evening. All six are in the system. All six were separated at different ages — some at birth. There is no family photo. No group image. No record of all six together in any file, in any county, in any state database. The drawing on the wall is the only existing image of all six children in one place. Nia drew it from memory — a memory she's been holding since she was four.
4. **Payoff (Consequence):** The advocate files an emergency sibling-connection petition. The "property damage" complaint is dismissed. The foster mother is counseled. And the photograph of the drawing is entered into each sibling's case file — six files, six counties, one image. A judge orders sibling visits to begin within ninety days. Nia gets a new sketchbook. On the first page, she draws the same six figures. This time she adds a seventh: herself, smiling.

**Character Lock:**
- Nia Carter — 9, Black female, small for her age, quiet, protective of her memories, crayon-stained fingers, defiant calm
- Foster mother (unnamed) — 45, white female, arms crossed, pointing at the wall, frustrated
- Advocate Lorraine Briggs — 37, Black female, professional cardigan, lanyard with county ID, clipboard, the one who notices
- Setting: Foster home hallway/bedroom, suburban Columbus OH, weekday afternoon, beige walls, the crayon drawing visible on one bedroom wall
- Object clue: the crayon drawing of six children holding hands — names written underneath each figure

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Nia stands small in the hallway, chin up, facing the foster mother who points angrily at the bedroom wall behind them — on the wall, a crayon drawing of six children holding hands is clearly visible, names written beneath each figure in a child's printing.
