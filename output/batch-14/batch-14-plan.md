### M141 | Job Interview Checkbox → HR VP's Same Box

**Source:** Concept 22 #1 — Prison, Parole & Second Chances  
**Engine:** E00 — Humiliation → Reveal  
**Opening Style:** Dialogue Slap

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (Humiliation):** An HR manager at a Phoenix logistics company publicly tosses a resume into the trash after seeing "yes" on the felony conviction question, muttering "we don't hire criminals" loud enough for the waiting room to hear.
2. **Build (Clue):** The applicant sits motionless, hands folded over a worn leather portfolio embossed with initials that match a framed portrait in the lobby hallway — the company's founding VP of Operations.
3. **Turn (Recognition):** The hiring VP steps out of her office having heard the commotion, retrieves the resume, and addresses the room: "I checked that same box twenty years ago."
4. **Payoff (Consequence):** The VP hires the man on the spot and reassigns the HR manager to mandatory bias training, revealing her own record and the company's second-chance hiring policy she personally wrote.

**Character Lock:**
- Marcus Tate — 34, close-cropped hair, navy polo, khaki slacks, leather portfolio with gold initials "MT"
- Diane Keller — 52, HR manager, blonde bob, gray blazer, reading glasses on a chain
- Vanessa Cole — 48, VP Operations, natural hair in a low bun, burgundy dress, small wrist tattoo of a compass
- Setting: Corporate lobby, Phoenix AZ, mid-morning fluorescent light
- Object clue: leather portfolio with embossed initials

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Marcus sits frozen in the waiting area as Diane drops his resume into the trash in front of other applicants, his leather portfolio visible on his lap.

### M142 | The Repainted Bench → Where He Proposed

**Source:** E04 #1 — The Long Wait / Devotion  
**Engine:** E04 — Long Wait / Devotion  
**Opening Style:** Countdown of Years

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (Ritual):** For 31 years, every first Saturday of spring, an elderly man in Charleston kneels beside a park bench with a can of forest-green paint and recoats it by hand, alone.
2. **Build (Deepening):** Joggers pass without noticing; only the groundskeeper watches, having never asked. This year the man's hands shake badly, paint dripping onto his knees, yet he won't accept help.
3. **Turn (Meaning revealed):** The groundskeeper finally approaches and sees a brass plaque hidden under layers of paint: a date, two names, and the words "She said yes here."
4. **Payoff (Poignant close):** The man finishes, touches the bench once, whispers a name, and walks slowly back to the assisted-living van — he's been keeping the bench alive for a wife who left the world three decades ago but never left this spot.

**Character Lock:**
- Henry Callahan — 79, thin white hair, paint-stained khaki jacket, trembling hands, worn wedding band
- Setting: Waterfront Park, Charleston SC, early spring morning, overcast soft light
- Object clue: brass plaque under layers of green paint

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Henry kneels alone beside the bench in pale morning light, brush in trembling hand, green paint on his knees, the bench half-finished.

### M143 | Open House Dismissal → Cash Buyers in Work Clothes

**Source:** Concept 17 #2 — Real Estate, Housing & Eviction  
**Engine:** E00 — Humiliation → Reveal  
**Opening Style:** Silent Witness

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (Humiliation):** At an upscale open house in Scottsdale, the listing agent visibly ignores a Latino couple in dusty work boots, steering wealthy-looking guests ahead of them with a smile.
2. **Build (Clue):** The couple quietly studies every room, the husband jotting notes on a folded blueprint; on the kitchen island sits the agent's "Offers received" sheet — blank.
3. **Turn (Recognition):** The couple's attorney arrives in a suit and announces they're submitting a full-price cash offer, referencing the three other homes in the neighborhood they already closed on this month.
4. **Payoff (Consequence):** The seller accepts on the spot; the agent loses the listing commission because the buyers brought their own representation, and neighborhood comps jump — powered by the couple's renovation business.

**Character Lock:**
- Rafael and Lucia Estrada — early 40s, sun-weathered skin, dusty Carhartts, Rafael carries a rolled blueprint
- Brenda Yates — 38, blonde highlights, stiletto heels, white blazer, Bluetooth earpiece
- Setting: Luxury open house, Scottsdale AZ, late afternoon golden-hour light through floor-to-ceiling windows
- Object clue: folded blueprint in Rafael's back pocket

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Brenda smiles past the Estradas toward a well-dressed couple, while Rafael and Lucia stand near the kitchen island studying the empty offers sheet, blueprint visible in his pocket.

### M144 | The Janitor's Chessboard → 19-Move Checkmate

**Source:** E09 #4 — Underdog Earns It  
**Engine:** E09 — Underdog Earns It  
**Opening Style:** Deadline Pressure

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (Underestimation):** A corporate chess club in a Chicago high-rise snickers when the building's night janitor sets down his mop bucket and asks to play the reigning company champion during lunch hour.
2. **Build (Pressure):** The champion agrees "for fun," expecting a quick game. Within eight moves the janitor's unconventional Sicilian variation forces the champion into defensive retreat; spectators stop eating.
3. **Turn (Skill silences):** Move 17 — the janitor sacrifices his queen. The room gasps. Two moves later the champion's king is trapped; checkmate in 19 total moves.
4. **Payoff (Earned triumph):** The janitor thanks them, picks up his mop, and returns to work. No hidden identity, no secret master title — just decades of studying borrowed books in empty lobbies after midnight.

**Character Lock:**
- Elroy "Roy" Simmons — 61, salt-and-pepper stubble, navy janitor jumpsuit, reading glasses tucked in pocket
- Derek Huang — 35, VP of analytics, tailored vest, company chess pin on lapel
- Setting: 40th-floor breakroom, Chicago IL, noon sunlight through glass walls, city skyline visible
- Object clue: a dog-eared paperback chess book peeking from the mop bucket

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Roy in his janitor jumpsuit studies the board mid-game, mop bucket beside the table, while Derek leans back wide-eyed and coworkers crowd behind them.

### M145 | The Unopened Voicemail → The Box Under the Stairs

**Source:** E06 #1 — Bittersweet / Too Late  
**Engine:** E06 — Bittersweet / Too Late  
**Opening Style:** Letter Fragment

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (Quiet dread):** Two weeks after his father's funeral in a small Ohio house, a son finally presses play on the last voicemail he'd been avoiding since the notification lit his phone during the service.
2. **Build (The message):** His father's tired voice says: "I know you're busy. I just wanted to tell you — check the box under the stairs. I kept every letter you sent. Even the ones you think I threw away."
3. **Turn (Too late):** The son finds the box — every birthday card, every hasty postcard, even the angry letter he mailed at 19 and regretted — all filed by date, some annotated in his father's hand with small hearts.
4. **Payoff (Grief + love):** He reads his father's note clipped to the last letter: "I read these when I miss you. Which is every day." There is no one left to call back.

**Character Lock:**
- James Whitfield — 42, loosened tie, stubble, dark circles, phone in hand
- Setting: Narrow hallway of a modest two-story house, Dayton OH, late evening, single lamp glow
- Object clue: the phone screen showing "1 Saved Voicemail — Dad"

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** James sits on the hallway stairs in the dim house, phone pressed to his ear, eyes closed, a cardboard box barely visible in the shadow beneath the staircase.

2. **Build (Clue):** The clown removes his red nose slowly; beneath the greasepaint is an older man whose hands carry the precise steadiness of a surgeon. A child in a wheelchair tugs the administrator's sleeve: "That's Dr. Charlie."

### M147 | The Unsent Postcards → A Promise to His Late Wife

**Source:** E02 #5 — Mystery → Explanation  
**Engine:** E02 — Mystery → Explanation  
**Opening Style:** Curious Ritual

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (Strange behavior):** A retired mailman in Santa Fe buys one postcard from every town he passes through on his road trips — Albuquerque, Flagstaff, Durango — but never mails a single one.
2. **Build (Curiosity deepens):** His granddaughter, helping him move, finds a shoebox with 200+ postcards, all addressed to "Maggie, 14 Juniper Lane," all stamped, none sent. The house at 14 Juniper was demolished years ago.
3. **Turn (The reason):** She asks. He explains: Maggie was her grandmother, who always wanted to travel but couldn't. He promised her he'd "send postcards from everywhere." She died before the first trip — so he writes them, but there's no one to receive them.
4. **Payoff (Warm/bittersweet):** The granddaughter reads the most recent card — dated last week — and it says: "Wish you were here, Mag. You'd love the light." She frames it for him. He smiles and says, "She knows."

**Character Lock:**
- Arthur Webb — 74, lean build, flannel shirt, canvas travel bag, reading glasses
- Sophie Webb — 26, granddaughter, dark ponytail, moving-day sweatshirt
- Setting: Arthur's small adobe house, Santa Fe NM, afternoon sun through dusty windows, moving boxes stacked
- Object clue: shoebox of 200+ addressed, stamped, unsent postcards

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Arthur sits at his kitchen table writing a postcard, a stamped stack beside him, none in the outgoing mail — Sophie visible in the doorway holding the discovered shoebox.

**Engine:** E00 — Humiliation → Reveal  
**Opening Style:** Room Goes Wrong

**4-Beat Arc:**

2. **Build (Clue):** The retriever wears a plain harness (no visible service patches from this angle); it paws insistently at its owner's hip, then sits and stares upward — a trained alert posture no bystander recognizes.

### M149 | The Stolen Idea → Timestamped Email

**Source:** E05 #14 — Comeuppance  
**Engine:** E05 — Comeuppance (the downfall)  
**Opening Style:** Dialogue Slap

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (Arrogance):** At a quarterly all-hands meeting in a San Francisco tech firm, a senior director presents a product roadmap to thunderous applause, calling it "months of my vision finally realized."
2. **Build (Self-made trap):** He soaks in praise, even thanking specific executives by name — unaware that in the third row sits the summer intern whose strategy deck, timestamped six weeks earlier, matches his slides nearly verbatim.
3. **Turn (Own behavior sinks him):** The intern doesn't speak. She doesn't have to — she already forwarded her original deck to the CEO the night before, subject line: "Thought you'd want to see the date on this." The CEO checks his phone mid-applause.
4. **Payoff (Self-inflicted fall):** The CEO calls the director into his office before the crowd disperses; by Friday the director is terminated for IP misrepresentation, and the intern is offered a full-time strategist role — her slides now credited on the company wiki.

**Character Lock:**
- Kevin Brash — 44, senior director, navy turtleneck, Apple Watch, confident posture at podium
- Amara Okafor — 22, intern, box braids, modest blazer, laptop open in third row
- Setting: All-hands auditorium, SoMa tech campus, San Francisco CA, stage lighting + screen projection
- Object clue: Amara's laptop screen showing the timestamped email in her sent folder

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Kevin gestures grandly at the projection screen while Amara sits in the third row, laptop open, expression calm — the sent-email timestamp glowing on her screen (not readable to viewer).

### M150 | The Widower and the Runaway → A Chosen Family

**Source:** E21 #3 — Found Family / Chosen Bond  
**Engine:** E21 — Found Family  
**Opening Style:** Tender Oddity

**4-Beat Arc:**

1. **Hook (Unlikely pairing):** A 72-year-old widower eating alone at a Memphis diner every night notices a 16-year-old girl sitting in the same corner booth after closing, nursing a single coffee and never leaving until the staff kicks her out.
2. **Build (Small acts):** He starts leaving an extra order of pancakes "by accident" at her booth. She never thanks him but always eats. One night he notices her backpack is packed full — she has nowhere to go.
3. **Turn (Bond forms):** He doesn't ask questions. He slides a house key across the table with a note: "Spare room's got clean sheets. Door locks from inside. No strings." She stares at it for ten minutes. She takes it.
4. **Payoff (Belonging):** Months later, the diner waitress sees them at the same booth — but now they're across from each other, arguing about crossword clues like family. His emergency-contact card at the clinic now reads her name.

**Character Lock:**
- Walter "Walt" Drummond — 72, broad shoulders gone soft, flannel shirt, bifocals, wedding ring still on
- Jaylen "Jay" Monroe — 16, oversized hoodie, box braids tucked under a beanie, stuffed backpack
- Setting: 24-hour diner, Midtown Memphis TN, late night, neon sign glow through window, vinyl booths
- Object clue: the brass house key on the table between them

**Peak-tension moment (photo):** Walt eats alone at his booth while Jay sits two booths away in her hoodie, backpack stuffed beside her, both illuminated by the diner's neon — separate but orbiting.
